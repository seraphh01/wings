# Wings — Founder Athlete Experiment v0.1

**Experiment:** Ten and Ten  
**Duration:** 6 weeks  
**Athlete:** Serafim  
**Schedule:** 4 training days per week  
**Primary goal:** 10 consecutive freestanding, head-to-floor handstand push-ups  
**Secondary goal:** 10 consecutive clean bar muscle-ups, with technique freedom  
**Status:** Ready for baseline calibration

---

## 1. Purpose

This is both a training block and the first end-to-end test of the Wings progression system.

The experiment must answer two questions:

1. Can Serafim reach 10 valid consecutive repetitions in each target movement within six weeks?
2. Can Wings capture enough structured evidence to explain what changed, why it changed, and what should happen next?

The product proof is broader than the final result. Even if one target is not reached, Wings succeeds as a prototype if it can reconstruct the journey, identify improvement, detect fatigue or plateaus, and produce a credible next recommendation.

---

## 2. Goal standards

### 2.1 Freestanding handstand push-up

A repetition is valid when all of the following are true:

1. The repetition begins in a freestanding handstand with elbows extended and both feet clear of any support.
2. The athlete descends under control until the crown of the head lightly contacts a consistent padded floor marker.
3. No wall, person, or external object assists the repetition.
4. The athlete presses back to a stable handstand with elbows visibly extended.
5. The feet remain off the floor between repetitions.
6. A small balance correction through the hands is permitted; walking away from the test area or placing a foot down ends the set.
7. The head contact is controlled. Impacting or bouncing the head invalidates the repetition and ends the attempt.

**Goal condition:** 10 valid consecutive repetitions captured in one unedited video.

### 2.2 Bar muscle-up

A repetition is valid when all of the following are true:

1. The repetition begins below the bar with both elbows extended.
2. The athlete moves from below the bar to a clear support position above it.
3. Both elbows reach visible extension in the support position.
4. No person, platform, or ground contact assists the repetition.
5. Kip, swing, and grip adjustment are permitted.
6. A pronounced one-arm chicken-wing transition is not considered a clean repetition.
7. The athlete remains on the bar for the entire set.

**Goal condition:** 10 valid consecutive repetitions captured in one unedited video.

### 2.3 Consistent testing conditions

For baseline, midpoint, and final tests, preserve:

- The same handstand surface and head-contact marker.
- The same pull-up bar where practical.
- Similar camera angle.
- Similar warm-up.
- Similar time of day where practical.
- At least 48 hours without hard training for the tested movement before the final test.

## 3. Starting information

Known prior performance:

- Handstand: approximately 40 seconds.
- Muscle-ups: approximately 5 consecutive repetitions.
- Handstand push-up maximum: not yet formally established.

The prior muscle-up result is useful context but is not the official baseline. Week 1 begins with standardized testing.

## 4. Baseline protocol

Complete the baseline across the first two training days rather than testing both maximums while fatigued.

### Day 1 — Handstand push-up baseline

#### Readiness record

- Sleep duration.
- Sleep quality from 1–5.
- General energy from 1–5.
- Wrist, elbow, and shoulder discomfort from 0–10.
- Body mass, optional but recommended.

#### Warm-up

1. 3–5 minutes of general movement.
2. Wrist preparation in pain-free ranges.
3. Scapular elevation and controlled shoulder movement.
4. Two easy wall handstand holds.
5. Two to four submaximal freestanding handstand entries.
6. One or two partial or single HSPU rehearsals, without fatigue.

#### Test

- Perform up to three maximum-quality attempts.
- Rest 5–7 minutes between attempts.
- Stop an attempt when the next repetition would break the standard.
- Record every attempt from the side-front angle.
- The best valid set becomes `H0`, the official HSPU baseline.

#### Additional capacity measures

- Best freestanding handstand hold.
- Maximum wall-facing HSPUs with the same head-to-floor depth, stopping with one repetition in reserve.
- One controlled HSPU eccentric duration.

### Day 2 — Muscle-up baseline

#### Readiness record

Use the same readiness fields as Day 1, adding hand and elbow condition.

#### Warm-up

1. 3–5 minutes of general movement.
2. Shoulder and scapular preparation.
3. Easy active hangs and scapular pull-ups.
4. Two progressive pull-up sets.
5. Two or three easy muscle-up singles.

#### Test

- Perform up to two maximum-quality attempts.
- Rest 7–10 minutes between attempts.
- Stop if grip or transition quality becomes unsafe.
- Record every attempt.
- The best valid set becomes `M0`, the official muscle-up baseline.

#### Additional capacity measures

- Maximum high pull height or chest-to-bar repetitions with one repetition in reserve.
- Weighted pull-up load for a controlled 3–5 repetition set, if already familiar with weighted pull-ups.
- Maximum straight-bar dips with two repetitions in reserve.

## 5. Training architecture

### Weekly schedule

| Day | Primary focus | Secondary exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | HSPU quality and vertical-push strength | Easy muscle-up technique |
| Day 2 | Muscle-up power and pulling strength | Easy handstand balance |
| Day 3 | HSPU volume and repeatability | Shoulder/scapular support |
| Day 4 | Muscle-up volume and repeatability | Easy HSPU singles or balance |

Recommended rhythm:

> **Monday — Tuesday — Thursday — Saturday**

Equivalent spacing is acceptable. Avoid placing all four sessions on consecutive days.

### Training rules

- Skill work happens before accessory strength work.
- Normal working sets stop with approximately 2–3 repetitions in reserve.
- Failure is reserved for scheduled tests, not daily training.
- Only repetitions that meet the current form standard count toward skill volume.
- Rest 3–5 minutes between demanding HSPU sets.
- Rest 3–5 minutes between muscle-up working sets; use longer rest for large sets.
- End high-skill work when balance or transition quality clearly deteriorates.
- Do not compensate for a missed session by doubling the next session.

## 6. Adaptive prescription rules

Because the official HSPU baseline is not yet known, Wings derives working prescriptions from `H0` and `M0`.

### 6.1 Main-set size

For each target movement:

- If maximum is **1–2 reps**, use quality singles.
- If maximum is **3–5 reps**, use sets of 1–2 reps.
- If maximum is **6–7 reps**, use sets of 2–4 reps.
- If maximum is **8–9 reps**, use sets of 3–5 reps and begin larger-set practice.
- At all levels, stop sets before form deterioration.

### 6.2 Volume progression

Increase weekly target-movement volume only when:

- At least 90% of planned repetitions were valid.
- Average session effort was no higher than 8/10.
- No joint discomfort trend is rising.
- Performance did not decline across two consecutive exposures.

When these conditions are met, increase target-movement volume by approximately 10–20% the following week.

### 6.3 Fatigue response

Reduce the next exposure's target-movement volume by approximately 30% if any of the following occurs:

- Valid repetitions fall more than 20% below the recent normal result.
- Two consecutive sessions feel harder while performance declines.
- Wrist, elbow, or shoulder discomfort reaches 4/10 or meaningfully changes technique.
- The athlete cannot maintain the exercise standard despite adequate rest.

Stop the movement and reassess if pain is sharp, increasing, causes weakness or instability, or persists outside training. The experiment is not a reason to train through injury.

## 7. Session templates

Accessory work should support the two goals, not turn each session into a full-body endurance test. Loads remain submaximal unless a test is scheduled.

### Day 1 — HSPU quality

1. **Freestanding handstand entry and balance practice:** 8–12 minutes, low fatigue.
2. **Freestanding HSPU:** 5–8 quality sets using the baseline-based set size.
3. **Wall-facing HSPU or controlled HSPU eccentric:** 3 sets of 3–8.
4. **Deficit pike push-up or overhead press equivalent:** 3 sets of 5–8.
5. **Scapular elevation in handstand or pike:** 2–3 sets of 8–12.
6. **Easy muscle-up exposure:** 3–5 singles, only if elbows and shoulders feel fresh.

### Day 2 — Muscle-up power

1. **Easy handstand balance:** 5–8 minutes.
2. **Bar muscle-up:** 5–8 quality sets using the baseline-based set size.
3. **High pull-up or chest-to-bar pull-up:** 3–4 sets of 3–5.
4. **Weighted pull-up:** 3–4 sets of 3–5, only if already technically familiar.
5. **Straight-bar dip:** 3 sets of 5–10.
6. **Transition drill:** 2–3 low-fatigue sets.

### Day 3 — HSPU volume and repeatability

1. **Handstand balance:** 8–10 minutes.
2. **Freestanding HSPU clusters:** accumulate the week's prescribed volume in small, repeatable sets.
3. **Wall-facing HSPU:** 3–4 sets, finishing with 2 repetitions in reserve.
4. **Slow eccentric HSPU:** 2–3 controlled repetitions total if recovery is good.
5. **Wrist and scapular capacity:** 2–3 easy sets.
6. **Optional easy pulling:** 2–3 sets, well below failure.

### Day 4 — Muscle-up volume and repeatability

1. **Bar muscle-up clusters:** accumulate the prescribed volume without grinding.
2. **Explosive pull-up:** 3 sets of 3–5.
3. **Straight-bar dip:** 3 sets of 6–10.
4. **Active hang or scapular pull-up:** 2–3 sets.
5. **Easy freestanding HSPU singles or balance:** 5–8 minutes, only while crisp.

## 8. Six-week progression

### Week 1 — Baseline and calibration

**Objective:** establish valid starting results and determine sustainable working-set sizes.

- Complete the two baseline tests.
- Use the remaining exposures to practice the standards.
- Keep accessory work conservative.
- Finish most work with 3 repetitions in reserve.
- Record video for every target-movement working set during calibration.

**Wings decision:** calculate `H0`, `M0`, starting volume, and the first readiness state.

### Week 2 — Quality accumulation

**Objective:** accumulate technically valid repetitions without approaching failure.

- Use small sets at approximately 40–60% of the current maximum.
- Aim for 10–20% more valid target repetitions than the comparable Week 1 training exposure.
- Preserve long rest and clean execution.
- Do not perform a maximum test.

**Wings decision:** increase volume only if validity, effort, and discomfort rules are satisfied.

### Week 3 — Capacity building

**Objective:** increase total valid volume and begin joining repetitions into larger sets.

- Retain one small-set quality exposure for each movement.
- On the volume exposure, make one or two sets one repetition larger than the normal working set.
- Keep all remaining sets submaximal.
- Film the first and last working set to compare deterioration.

**Wings decision:** determine whether the limiting factor is strength, balance/transition, or repeatability.

### Week 4 — Intensification and checkpoint

**Objective:** practice larger sets while preserving technique.

- Reduce accessory volume slightly.
- Early in the week, use normal small-set quality work.
- On the second exposure, perform one checkpoint set after warming up.
- Stop the checkpoint with approximately one repetition in reserve; it is not a true maximum.
- Follow it with reduced-volume back-off work.

**Expected checkpoint direction:** a larger valid set than baseline or the same set at visibly better quality and lower effort.

**Wings decision:** continue, adjust, or flag a plateau based on four weeks of evidence.

### Week 5 — Goal specificity

**Objective:** become comfortable with longer uninterrupted sets.

- Use fewer total sets but include one or two larger submaximal sets.
- Practice the exact test setup and camera angle.
- Maintain only the accessory work needed to preserve strength.
- Do not add new exercises.
- Finish the last demanding exposure at least 72 hours before Week 6's first test rehearsal.

**Wings decision:** estimate current goal probability and select test order based on recovery and checkpoint results.

### Week 6 — Taper and final tests

**Objective:** express the capacity built during the block.

- Reduce total target-movement volume by approximately 50–60%.
- Keep a few crisp singles or small sets early in the week.
- Remove fatiguing accessory work.
- Test the two movements on separate days.
- Allow at least 48 hours between final tests.
- Allow one primary attempt and, only if the first ended for a technical mistake rather than fatigue, one additional attempt after full rest.

Suggested order:

1. Test the movement with the better Week 4 checkpoint first.
2. Test the other movement 48–72 hours later.

## 9. Per-session Wings record

### Pre-session

- Date and time.
- Session type.
- Sleep duration.
- Sleep quality: 1–5.
- Energy: 1–5.
- Motivation: 1–5.
- Wrist discomfort: 0–10.
- Elbow discomfort: 0–10.
- Shoulder discomfort: 0–10.
- Previous relevant training date.

### For every target-movement set

- Exercise variation.
- Attempted repetitions.
- Valid repetitions.
- Invalid repetition reason.
- Assistance or resistance.
- Rest before set.
- Effort: RPE 1–10 or repetitions in reserve.
- Video attached: yes/no.
- Technique note.

### Post-session

- Session completed: full/partial/abandoned.
- Total valid target repetitions.
- Best valid set.
- Average effort.
- Any pain increase.
- Main limiter.
- Athlete note.
- Confidence toward goal: 1–5.

### Limiter options

For HSPU:

- Entry consistency.
- Balance.
- Bottom-position strength.
- Pressing strength.
- Lockout stability.
- Fear or hesitation.
- Wrist/shoulder discomfort.

For muscle-ups:

- Grip.
- Pull height.
- Transition timing.
- Dip/lockout strength.
- Swing rhythm.
- Cardiovascular fatigue.
- Elbow/shoulder discomfort.

## 10. Wings progression states for this experiment

Each goal moves independently through these states:

1. **Baseline pending**
2. **Calibrated**
3. **Building quality volume**
4. **Building set capacity**
5. **Ready for checkpoint**
6. **Ready for goal attempt**
7. **Goal achieved**
8. **Goal consolidated**
9. **Needs load adjustment**
10. **Needs physical reassessment**

### Ready for goal attempt

Wings may recommend a final attempt when:

- The athlete has completed at least 90% of planned target sessions in the previous two weeks.
- No blocking discomfort is active.
- The checkpoint and recent working sets show positive or stable performance.
- The athlete has practiced a set at or above approximately 70–80% of the target without technical breakdown.
- Fatigue has been reduced before testing.

### Goal achieved

The state changes to achieved only when 10 consecutive valid repetitions meeting the written standard are recorded.

### Goal consolidated

The state changes to consolidated after the athlete repeats the 10-repetition result in a later session. Consolidation is not required for the six-week binary goal but remains part of the Wings progression model.

## 11. Weekly review generated by Wings

At the end of each week, Wings should answer:

1. How many sessions were planned and completed?
2. How many valid HSPU and muscle-up repetitions were recorded?
3. What was the best valid set for each movement?
4. Did volume, set size, or execution quality improve?
5. What was the most frequent limiter?
6. Is fatigue or discomfort trending upward?
7. What should change next week, and why?

Example:

> “You completed all four sessions. Your best freestanding HSPU set remained at 4, but valid weekly volume increased from 14 to 20 repetitions with stable effort. Balance caused most invalid reps, so next week retains the same strength volume and adds short, fresh balance exposures. Your best muscle-up set improved from 5 to 6, and your transition remained clean. Increase muscle-up training volume by 10%, without adding another maximum set.”

## 12. Experiment success criteria

### Athletic outcomes

| Outcome | HSPU | Muscle-up |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full success | 10 valid consecutive reps | 10 valid consecutive reps |
| Strong progress | At least 75% improvement from baseline | At least 75% improvement from baseline |
| Meaningful progress | At least 40% improvement from baseline | At least 40% improvement from baseline |
| No measurable progress | Best valid set unchanged | Best valid set unchanged |

Percentage outcomes are secondary. The official goals remain 10 and 10.

### Product outcomes

The Wings prototype succeeds if it can:

- Store every planned and completed session.
- Separate attempted from valid repetitions.
- Reconstruct volume and best-set trends.
- Explain state changes.
- Identify the main limiter for each movement.
- Detect fatigue or discomfort rules.
- Recommend the next week's load transparently.
- Generate a credible six-week story without manual spreadsheet reconstruction.

## 13. End-of-experiment report

The final report should contain:

1. Baseline and final results.
2. Goal-standard videos.
3. Weekly best-set chart.
4. Weekly valid-volume chart.
5. Completion and consistency rate.
6. Effort and discomfort trends.
7. Most common limiter by movement.
8. Every Wings state transition and its reason.
9. What the recommendation engine got right or wrong.
10. Product changes required before beginner testing.

The closing narrative should be understandable without reading the raw logs:

> “Over six weeks, Serafim trained four days per week toward two ten-repetition goals. Wings recorded the work, distinguished valid performance from attempts, adapted volume to recovery, and explained each progression decision. The final result demonstrates both the athlete's change and the product's ability to guide that change.”
