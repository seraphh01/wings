**Status:** Working foundation  
**Date:** 15 September 2026  
**Founder Athlete:** Serafim  
**Motto:** *Calisthenics fuels your life.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

Wings is a calisthenics progression and training community that helps people move from their first controlled repetition to advanced skills.

It brings together three experiences that are normally fragmented across workout trackers, tutorial platforms, social media, messaging groups, and local communities:

1. **Progression:** understand where you are and what comes next.
2. **Training:** turn goals into sessions and measurable improvement.
3. **Togetherness:** find people who can train, encourage, and grow with you.

Wings is not primarily a social feed, an exercise encyclopedia, or a generic workout logger. Its central promise is:

> **Know where you are. Know what comes next. Never train alone unless you choose to.**

The first product must prove that Wings can guide an athlete through a complete progression loop:

> **Assess → choose a goal → follow a path → train → record evidence → validate progress → unlock the next step.**

Serafim will be the first longitudinal test case and public proof of concept. His experience will validate advanced-skill tracking, training logs, progression rules, and the founder story. Beginner and intermediate testers will then be required to prevent the product from being designed only around an advanced athlete.

## 2. The problem

Calisthenics information is abundant, but the athlete's journey is fragmented.

A typical athlete must currently use:

- YouTube or social media to discover exercises.
- Notes or spreadsheets to track workouts.
- Generic fitness applications to record repetitions.
- Messaging groups to organize shared sessions.
- Memory and intuition to decide when to change progressions.
- Public social feeds for motivation, often without useful context.

This creates several recurring problems:

- Beginners do not know where to start.
- Athletes attempt skills without understanding prerequisites.
- Intermediate athletes plateau without knowing why.
- Progress is reduced to isolated personal records instead of a coherent journey.
- People train alone even when compatible athletes are nearby.
- Advice mixes evidence, opinion, and personal experience without distinction.
- Social comparison rewards spectacle more than patient improvement.

The opportunity is not simply to collect more calisthenics content. It is to connect knowledge, training, evidence, and people into one guided system.

## 3. Vision, mission, and product promise

### Vision

A world in which every person interested in calisthenics has a clear path, supportive people, and the confidence to progress.

### Mission

Help athletes understand their current ability, train toward meaningful goals, prove their progress, and build relationships through calisthenics.

### Product promise

When an athlete opens Wings, the product should help answer four questions:

1. Where am I now?
2. What am I working toward?
3. What should I train today?
4. Who can help me along the way?

### Brand meaning

The name **Wings** represents earned freedom. An athlete begins grounded, builds strength and control, and gradually becomes capable of movements that once appeared impossible.

The wings are not awarded for being better than other people. They are earned through patience, discipline, courage, and consistent personal growth.

## 4. Product principles

### 4.1 Progress is personal

Wings compares an athlete primarily with their previous self. Public rankings must never become the main definition of success.

### 4.2 Progress must be meaningful

Finishing a workout does not automatically mean the athlete advanced. Progress is based on measurable improvement in performance, execution, consistency, or progression difficulty.

### 4.3 Technique is part of the result

Ten poor repetitions are not equivalent to ten controlled repetitions. Range of motion, body position, tempo, and control belong in the progression model.

### 4.4 Community should cause action

Community features should help people train, learn, encourage, or contribute. Passive scrolling is not a core product outcome.

### 4.5 Every level deserves respect

A first push-up can represent as much personal growth as an advanced planche. Language, achievements, and community reactions should reflect this.

### 4.6 Guidance should be understandable

Recommendations should state why an exercise, progression, or adjustment was selected. The athlete remains in control.

### 4.7 Truth has context

Training content should distinguish between established knowledge, study-backed claims, coaching interpretation, and personal experience.

### 4.8 Training fuels life

Wings should reinforce qualities that extend beyond training: patience, self-respect, reliability, courage, friendship, and service to others.

## 5. Target users

### Primary audience

People aged approximately 16–40 who are interested in bodyweight training and want more direction or connection than a generic fitness application provides.

### Initial user types

#### The Grounded Beginner

- Cannot yet perform several fundamental movements with control.
- Is overwhelmed by conflicting information.
- Needs safety, encouragement, and a short first path.
- Success: completes the first week and understands the next milestone.

#### The Progressing Athlete

- Can perform fundamentals but is stuck between progressions.
- Follows tutorials from multiple sources without a coherent plan.
- Needs prerequisites, structured practice, and plateau diagnosis.
- Success: reaches a measurable skill or strength improvement.

#### The Advanced Practitioner

- Works on difficult static or dynamic skills.
- Needs precise volume, hold quality, video evidence, and fatigue management.
- May contribute knowledge or mentor others.
- Success: improves a specific progression without losing healthy balance.

#### The Social Athlete

- Is more consistent when training with others.
- Needs compatible partners, sessions, and accountability.
- Success: joins and attends useful training sessions.

#### The Returning Athlete

- Has previous ability but reduced current capacity after a break or injury.
- Needs respectful reassessment rather than being treated as a complete beginner.
- Success: rebuilds consistency and capacity safely.

## 6. The core user journey

### Stage 1 — Arrive

The user understands that Wings offers a path, not merely workouts. Account creation should require only the information needed to begin.

### Stage 2 — Assess

The athlete completes a short ability and context assessment:

- Training experience.
- Available equipment.
- Fundamental movement capacity.
- Current or previous pain limitations.
- Training availability.
- Preferred training environment.
- Interest in training with others.

The assessment should produce an understandable starting profile, not an intimidating universal score.

### Stage 3 — Choose a direction

The user selects one primary goal and optionally one supporting goal. Examples:

- First pull-up.
- Ten controlled pull-ups.
- Handstand.
- Muscle-up.
- Front lever.
- Planche.
- General strength and consistency.

The application explains why limiting active goals can improve progress.

### Stage 4 — See the path

Wings shows:

- The current progression.
- Relevant prerequisites.
- Supporting exercises.
- The criteria for moving forward.
- A realistic first milestone.

### Stage 5 — Train

The athlete receives or creates a session, performs it alone or with others, and records useful results without excessive friction.

### Stage 6 — Reflect

After training, Wings asks for only the information that affects future decisions:

- What was completed?
- How difficult was it?
- Was execution controlled?
- Was there pain or unusual discomfort?
- Is there a useful note or video?

### Stage 7 — Validate progress

Wings detects personal records and readiness signals. The athlete sees exactly what improved and what remains before the next progression.

### Stage 8 — Continue or contribute

The athlete starts the next cycle, trains with somebody, shares a meaningful milestone, or helps a less experienced member.

## 8. Progression engine v0.1

### 8.1 Progress is multidimensional

Wings should not collapse the whole athlete into a single level.

| Dimension | Example measurements |
|---|---|
| Strength | Repetitions, resistance, assistance, progression difficulty |
| Static control | Hold duration, position quality, repeatability |
| Technique | Range of motion, alignment, tempo, control |
| Endurance | Total volume, density, work capacity |
| Mobility | Relevant joint range and position access |
| Consistency | Meaningful training frequency over time |
| Knowledge | Understanding technique and programming |
| Community | Attendance, reliability, encouragement, contribution |

### 8.2 Exercise definition

Each exercise or skill variation requires:

- Name and category.
- Movement pattern.
- Difficulty band.
- Required equipment.
- Primary and secondary muscle groups.
- Prerequisites.
- Easier and harder variations.
- Performance type: repetitions, hold, distance, time, or load.
- Minimum form standard.
- Progression criteria.
- Regression conditions.
- Common mistakes.
- Safe alternatives.

### 8.3 Progression structure

Progressions form a directed graph, not one universal ladder. One skill may have multiple preparation paths, and one exercise may support several skills.

Example path:

> Incline push-up → push-up → diamond push-up → archer push-up → assisted one-arm push-up → one-arm push-up

The application may recommend a path, but athletes and coaches should be able to choose a valid alternative.

### 8.4 Athlete progression states

For a specific exercise progression, an athlete can be:

1. **Not assessed** — no useful evidence exists.
2. **Preparing** — prerequisites or supporting capacity are being developed.
3. **Ready to attempt** — prerequisite criteria are met.
4. **In progress** — the athlete is practicing the movement.
5. **Achieved** — the minimum standard has been demonstrated.
6. **Consolidated** — performance is repeatable across multiple sessions.
7. **Needs reassessment** — current evidence indicates meaningful regression, pain, or a long interruption.

“Achieved” and “consolidated” should remain distinct. A first successful attempt matters, but it is not the same as dependable ability.

### 8.5 Evidence levels

Initial evidence can be:

- Self-reported result.
- Logged result across one or more workouts.
- Video attached by the athlete.
- Peer-confirmed performance.
- Coach-reviewed performance.

The MVP should support self-reported and logged results. Video and external validation can follow without changing the underlying model.

### 8.6 Readiness rule

The first rules engine should be explicit and deterministic.

A user becomes **ready to attempt** a progression when:

- All mandatory prerequisites meet their minimum thresholds.
- The supporting movement has been performed with acceptable form.
- The relevant workload has been repeated across the configured number of sessions.
- No blocking pain or safety flag is active.

A user **achieves** a progression when the minimum performance and form standard is recorded.

A user **consolidates** it when the standard is repeated across multiple sessions within a defined period.

### 8.7 Recommendation logic

Version 0.1 should recommend the next action using this order:

1. Address a blocking prerequisite.
2. Continue the current progression if improvement is occurring.
3. Adjust volume, assistance, or variation when progress has stalled.
4. Consolidate a newly achieved progression.
5. Unlock the next progression when criteria are satisfied.
6. Recommend reassessment when pain, regression, or a long interruption is recorded.

The recommendation must include a short explanation, such as:

> “Continue tuck front lever this week. Your longest hold improved, but you need two more sessions with three controlled 12-second holds before attempting advanced tuck.”

## 9. MVP definition

### Core hypothesis

If Wings gives athletes a visible and credible path from current ability to a chosen goal, they will train more consistently and understand their progression better than when using disconnected tutorials and generic workout logs.

### Required MVP capabilities

1. Account and athlete profile.
2. Initial ability assessment.
3. Selection of one primary and one supporting goal.
4. Curated exercise and progression library for the selected pilot skills.
5. Personalized progression path.
6. Workout creation and assignment.
7. Active workout and result logging.
8. Personal-record detection.
9. Progression readiness and achievement states.
10. Progress timeline.
11. Simple shared-session creation and invitation.
12. Founder Athlete profile and public proof-of-concept story.

### Pilot skill coverage

The first content set should be intentionally narrow:

- Push-up foundation.
- Pull-up foundation.
- Handstand.
- Muscle-up.
- Front lever.
- Planche.

This covers beginner, intermediate, and advanced paths while remaining possible to curate properly.

### Explicitly outside the MVP

- Infinite public content feed.
- Livestreaming.
- Global competitive leaderboards.
- Automated video form analysis.
- Fully autonomous AI coach.
- Nutrition tracking.
- Marketplace or payment system.
- Paid coaching management.
- Full club and event administration.
- Large achievement economy.

## 12. Founder Athlete proof of concept

### Starting profile

Serafim's currently reported capabilities include:

- 50 push-ups.
- 15 pull-ups.
- 5 muscle-ups.
- Approximately 40-second handstand.
- Approximately 30-second advanced tuck planche.
- Approximately 30-second advanced tuck front lever.
- L-sit and dragon flag experience.

These results must be reassessed using the form criteria selected for Wings so the proof of concept begins from consistent evidence rather than memory alone.

### Six-week experiment

Select two goals:

- One primary advanced static-skill goal.
- One supporting capacity or technique goal.

For every relevant session, capture:

- Planned and completed exercises.
- Sets, repetitions, holds, resistance, assistance, and tempo.
- Longest valid performance.
- Total effective volume.
- Perceived effort.
- Readiness before training.
- Form note.
- Pain or unusual discomfort.
- Optional weekly comparison video.
- Missed session reason.

### Weekly review

Wings should generate a short narrative:

- What improved?
- What remained stable?
- What was missed?
- Is the athlete ready to progress?
- What is the recommended focus for next week, and why?

### Proof-of-concept success conditions

The experiment succeeds if Wings can:

1. Reconstruct the complete six-week training story.
2. Detect meaningful performance improvements.
3. Explain whether a progression should change.
4. Produce a useful next-week recommendation.
5. Do so with less manual interpretation than notes or a spreadsheet.

## 13. First external validation cohort

After the founder experiment begins, recruit at least five additional testers:

1. A complete beginner.
2. A beginner woman.
3. An intermediate athlete experiencing a plateau.
4. An athlete returning after a break.
5. A socially motivated athlete looking for partners.

Each tester should use Wings for at least four weeks. The goal is not statistical proof; it is to reveal incorrect assumptions, missing progression paths, confusing language, and excessive logging friction.

## 14. Success metrics

### Activation

- Assessment completion rate.
- First goal selection rate.
- First progression path viewed.
- First workout started and completed.

### Engagement

- Weekly active training athletes.
- Planned workout completion rate.
- Results logged per completed session.
- Four-week and six-week retention.

### Progression

- Athletes recording a measurable improvement.
- Athletes reaching `ready to attempt`, `achieved`, or `consolidated`.
- Athletes who can correctly identify their next progression and why.
- Time from assessment to first meaningful milestone.

### Togetherness

- Shared sessions created.
- Invitation acceptance rate.
- Confirmed attendance rate.
- Repeat training-partner sessions.

### North-star metric

> **Weekly Progressing Athletes:** athletes who completed meaningful training during the week and either improved a measured performance, advanced a progression state, or maintained the planned consistency required for their goal.

This metric recognizes that progress is not always a new record. Consistency and consolidation are legitimate parts of the journey.

## 15. Risks and safeguards

| Risk | Early safeguard |
|---|---|
| The founder's advanced experience biases the product | Test every major flow with beginner and returning-athlete cases |
| Progression rules imply medical certainty | Use clear training guidance, pain flags, and appropriate professional referral language |
| Logging becomes exhausting | Capture only data that changes feedback or recommendations |
| Users chase progression labels with poor form | Include form criteria and distinguish achieved from consolidated |
| Community becomes performative or hostile | Reward reliability, encouragement, and contribution rather than attention alone |
| The scope grows into every fitness category | Keep MVP focused on the core progression loop and six pilot paths |
| AI recommendations feel arbitrary | Begin with transparent deterministic rules and explanations |
| Local sessions create privacy or safety concerns | Use approximate location, controlled visibility, reporting, and consent-based contact |

| Primary identity | Personal progression, supported by community |
| First platform | Mobile-first application |
| Goal limit | One primary and one supporting active goal |
| Progression model | Branching graph with explicit prerequisites |
| Progress validation | Logged results first; richer evidence later |
| Recommendations | Deterministic and explainable before AI-driven |
| Social model | Training and contribution before content consumption |
| Competition | Personal records and cooperative challenges before global rankings |
| Content scope | Six curated pilot paths before broad exercise coverage |
| Founder role | First longitudinal test case, not universal persona |

## 19. One-sentence test

Every proposed feature should pass this question:

> **Does this help an athlete understand their path, train meaningfully, prove progress, or grow with other people?**

If the answer is no, it does not belong in the early Wings product.
